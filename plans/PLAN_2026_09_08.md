# Plan to win — PRC Data Challenge 2026 · written 2026-09-08

Supersedes the strategy in `RESEARCH_2026_09_08.md`, which was wrong on three material points.
Those corrections come first, because the plan only makes sense once they are on the table.

---

## 0. What the research memo got wrong, corrected against evidence

An adversarial review plus direct verification against the data and the live API refuted three
claims. Each is corrected here rather than quietly dropped.

**Wrong: "prize award is not by RMSE."** The rules page says plainly, *"We reserved a total of
5000 EUR for the first 3 ranking teams."* The 2025 prizes went 2500 / 1750 / 750 in strict rank
order, and third place received its prize while carrying the harshest methodological review in
the top five. Documentation, open source and originality are an **eligibility turnstile**, not a
ranking lever: *"You reach the Final only if you shared the (documented) code and docs in a
GitHub repository."* The memo cited the field's strongest counter-example as supporting evidence.
**Correction: RMSE rank is the prize. Documentation is a hard floor with no upside above it.**

**Wrong: the as-of-T design.** Verified on `ranking.parquet`:

| column, on the 344,841 rows we must predict | null |
|---|---|
| `BLOCK_TIME_UTC_mvt` — off-block, the pre-registered anchor T | **100.00%** |
| `MVT_TIME_UTC_mvt` — take-off | **0.00%** |
| `SCHED_TIME_UTC_mvt` | 0.00% |
| `AOBT_3_flt` — Network Manager actual off-block | 1.53% |

Off-block is withheld, necessarily, since taxi-out is take-off minus off-block. **The
pre-registration's as-of-T rule is anchored to a timestamp that exists for none of the rows we
must predict.** Take-off time, by contrast, is given exactly.

**Wrong: the noise floor.** RESULT 1 concluded the tail was irreducible and shared, so
leaderboard gaps would compress. The live leaderboard refutes it: the leader is at **260.93
seconds**, well under half the 627.7 my own ladder achieves, and the top four are inside 2.3% of
each other. The arithmetic in RESULT 1 was right; the inference was not.

## 1. The actual structure of this problem

The competition is not "forecast taxi-out from congestion." It is **denoise a second, independent
measurement of the answer.**

Two organisations time the same movement. The airport records off-block and take-off; the
Network Manager separately records `AOBT_3_flt`. Take-off is given. So for 98.9% of rows,
`MVT_TIME_UTC_mvt − AOBT_3_flt` is an estimate of the target from a different source.

Measured on all 2,084,659 admissible 2025 departures:

| estimator | coverage | RMSE vs true taxi-out |
|---|---|---|
| free-answer ladder, best rung (held-out Jan+Jul) | 100% | 627.7 s |
| `MVT_TIME − LOBT_flt` | 98.93% | 740.1 s |
| `MVT_TIME − EOBT_1_flt` | 98.93% | 676.6 s |
| **`MVT_TIME − AOBT_3_flt`** | **98.93%** | **384.8 s** |
| live leaderboard leader | | **260.9 s** |

Median absolute error of the AOBT proxy is 175 seconds against an RMSE of 384.8, so the
distribution is heavily skewed: the proxy is usually close and occasionally very wrong. **That is
a denoising problem with a known structure, and it is where the remaining distance to 260 lives.**

This reframing is the plan. Everything below serves it.

---

## 2. Where the error actually is

Two strata carry error out of all proportion to their size, and both are flaggable at prediction
time.

**The Network-Manager-unmatched stratum.** 1.07% of training departures have no `AOBT_3_flt`.
They carry **42.2% of total squared error**, have a beyond-three-hours rate 1,079 times the
matched rows, and a global-mean RMSE of 3,413 seconds against 417 for matched rows. In the
evaluation file the flag is 98.47% populated, so **this stratum is fully identifiable before
prediction**. Under squared loss, giving a stratum its own conditional prediction instead of the
global mean is exactly how that 42.2% is harvested.

**The long tail.** 202 of 2.08 million rows exceed three hours and carry 38% of squared error.
Unpredictable individually; the response is a robustness decision, not a prediction one.

## 3. The build, in order, with a kill condition on each step

Thirty-three days remain. The order is chosen so that each step is worthless to skip and cheap to
abandon.

**Step 1 — Amend the pre-registration to the real anchor. (day 1)**
Re-anchor every feature from off-block to **take-off**, which is known. Off-block becomes a
*predicted* quantity, not an input. Restate the leakage rule: a feature may use anything
knowable at take-off time, and the planted-future test is rewritten against that anchor.
*Kill: none. This is a correction, not an experiment.*

**Step 2 — The proxy baseline, submitted. (day 1–2)**
Predict `MVT_TIME − AOBT_3_flt`, falling back to the ladder where the proxy is absent. Submit it.
*Success: leaderboard confirms roughly 385, validating that our local evaluation tracks theirs.
Kill: if the leaderboard disagrees with local by more than 5%, stop and find out why before
building anything on top.* This step exists to calibrate the harness, not to score.

**Step 3 — Model the correction, not the target. (day 3–10)**
Target becomes `true_taxi_out − proxy`, the residual of the Network Manager's estimate. Gradient
boosting over: airport, runway, stand, aircraft type, operator, market segment, hour, day of
week, month, and the ladder's unimpeded time. The model learns *when and by how much the two
organisations disagree*, which is a far easier function than taxi-out itself.
*Success: beats 384.8 on held-out months. Kill: if it cannot beat the raw proxy, the residual is
noise and we fall back to step 2 plus stratification only.*

**Step 4 — The unmatched stratum. (day 8–12)**
Fit that 1.07% separately, since it has different physics and 42.2% of the error. Its own model,
its own report.
*Success: measurable RMSE reduction on the whole evaluation set. Kill: none, this is arithmetic.*

**Step 5 — Congestion features, anchored at take-off. (day 10–18)**
Departures taking off within windows around T; arrivals landing and going on-block near T, all of
which the evaluation file supplies in full. Queue position within the airport-runway stream.
*Success: further reduction beyond step 3. Kill: if it adds under 1%, stop and spend the days on
step 6 instead.*

**Step 6 — Weather, only if steps 3 to 5 leave room. (day 18–24)**
METAR for ten airports. **Explicitly deprioritised**: the 2025 sixth-placed team used weather and
the judges recorded that it was "ultimately found to have limited influence." Treat as a
hypothesis with a kill date, not a plan.
*Kill: under 1% improvement, drop it and say so in the paper.*

**Step 7 — Ensemble and calibration. (day 24–29)**
Cheap increment, last, never a strategy. Two years running a single well-fed booster beat
ensembles.

**Step 8 — Repository, paper, and the eligibility floor. (throughout, finished by day 31)**
GPLv3, documented, reproducible, no hardcoded paths, one entry point. This does not win, but its
absence disqualifies. Written as we go.

**Reserve: days 30–33.** No new ideas. Only re-verification, the final submission, and the
manifest check, because the evaluation objects were regenerated once already.

## 4. Validation, and the one thing that must not go wrong

Hold out whole months, group by airport-day, always report January and July separately, and
**weight the headline by the evaluation cell counts**, because July is both the larger month and
the harder one, 681 seconds against January's 554.

The submission budget is five per day. That is ample for honest calibration and far too little
for leaderboard fitting, which the organisers say they monitor. **The pre-registered planted-leak
test as written required observing that the leaderboard does not move, which spends submissions
probing the ranking process.** That is now replaced: the leak harness runs entirely against
held-out months locally, and the leaderboard is used only to confirm that local and remote agree.

**A new leakage class this plan introduces, and the guard for it.** Modelling the residual, and
using a label-derived unimpeded time, are both target encodings. The pre-registration's leak
discipline is purely temporal and cannot see them. Every label-derived statistic must therefore
be fitted inside the fold, never across it, with a test that plants a fold-crossing statistic and
must go red.

## 5. What winning requires, stated honestly

The leader is at 260.93 and second is 0.69% behind. We are at 384.8 with a single subtraction and
have not yet fitted anything. The distance from 385 to 260 is 32%, which is large but is
precisely the shape of a well-specified residual problem with a 42% stratum sitting unexploited.

**A realistic outcome is top five. Winning requires that the correction model plus the unmatched
stratum together close most of that 32%, and it requires no one at the top finding a further
lever we have not seen.** That is a real chance, not a certainty, and this plan is written to
find out quickly rather than to hope slowly.

The floor is worth naming too: finishing at all, with a documented reproducible repository and a
paper, puts us ahead of the 70% of registrants who never submit, and satisfies the eligibility
gate that several better-scoring teams have failed in past editions.

## 6. Verified competitive position, 2026-09-08 14:11Z

Read from the official API (`datacomp.opensky-network.org`, no auth) and cross-checked against an
independent mirror; the two agree exactly.

**305 teams registered, 71 have scored, 414 submissions.** Every score is on the same 344,841
departures we hold.

| # | team | best RMSE | subs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | enthusiastic-daisy | **260.93** | 10 |
| 2 | youthful-giraffe | 262.74 | 11 |
| 3 | quick-boat | 266.84 | 19 |
| 4 | upstanding-firefly | 267.01 | 4 |
| 5 | upbeat-goblin | 276.48 | 13 |
| 36 (median) | funny-jewel | 329.45 | 8 |
| 71 (last) | nice-umbrella | 660.53 | 7 |

**Frontier progression** from first submission on 4 September: 610 → 327 the same morning → 281
by end of day one → 277 → 271 → 266 → 261 → 260.93 on 7 September, and **nothing has beaten it
since**. The field collapsed to about 277 within eight hours of opening and has ground out
roughly 16 seconds in four days. A competent first-day gradient booster lands near 277 to 290.
The frontier is moving 1 to 2 seconds per day.

**Four rule facts that change the plan.**

**There is no phase two this year.** 2024 and 2025 both had a separate final submission set,
explicitly "to avoid the possibility of leaning from the leaderboard results." 2026 has one
ranking set and **the leaderboard score is the final score**. The only counterweight is a stated
intention to monitor for exploitation. Practical reading: submissions are a legitimate and
valuable signal within the five-per-day cap, and deliberate probing of the ranking mechanism is
not. We use them to confirm agreement, not to search.

**The evaluation set already changed once**, on 4 September, from 215,876 rows to 344,841. Every
pre-4-September score is incomparable. This is exactly why the fetch manifest exists; re-run it
before every submission.

**Local validation is far noisier than the leaderboard gaps.** A competitor publishing their
working measured a 95% bootstrap interval about 159 seconds wide on untrimmed held-out RMSE, and
found a paired comparison whose sign was *backwards* against the leaderboard. Dropping the worst
100 rows made the same comparison decisive and correct. **Therefore: select on trimmed RMSE,
report untrimmed.** Choosing between two candidate models on plain held-out RMSE, with gaps of a
few seconds, is a coin toss.

**Seed averaging is free money and is quantified.** The 2024 winner published it: one model
1,612, ten models 1,564, twenty models 1,562. **One to ten buys 3.0%**, ten to twenty buys 0.16%.
Average ten seeds; stop there.

**Calibration of what is achievable.** Published taxi-out RMSE of 96 to 200 seconds all comes
from single airports with surface surveillance or A-CDM queue state available at prediction time.
The closest analogue with our feature set, a Chalmers study on one airport with 14,382
departures, reached 186 to 198 seconds. Across ten heterogeneous airports and 344,841 rows, 261
is not obviously far from the ceiling; expect the winner to end somewhere near 230 to 250.

**Weather is now sourced and verified.** Iowa State's archive, public domain, 48 observations a
day at all ten airports for both January and July 2026, carrying visibility, weather codes and
precipitation — the de-icing and low-visibility signal. Archive the files locally: the 2024
winner was bitten when the upstream data was back-corrected mid-competition.

**Amendment to step 6.** Weather moves from "only if there is room" to a scheduled step, because
the source is confirmed and the January half of the evaluation is exactly where visibility and
de-icing bite. It stays behind the same one-per-cent kill condition.

**Amendment to step 7.** Seed averaging moves from "cheap increment, last" to a required step,
because it is a measured three per cent for no modelling risk.

**One more measured lever, from a competitor's published notes and consistent with our own
RESULT 1:** capping the target at its 99.9th percentile moved their airport-by-hour baseline from
507.7 to 366.4 seconds. Under squared loss the tail treatment is not a footnote; it is a first-
order decision, and it is tested in step 3, not deferred.

## 7. CORRECTIONS after Protocol B · 2026-09-08

An integration audit reproduced every measurement in this plan against the data and found the
headline comparison invalid. The corrections supersede §1, §2 and §3.

### 7.1 The headline was a denominator mismatch. The proxy loses.

§1 compared 627.7 (the ladder, over **all** held-out rows, one fold) against 384.8 (the proxy,
over the **98.9% covered subset**, across **all twelve months, in sample**). Two different row
sets and two different folds. Like for like — same rows, same held-out January and July:

| held-out Jan+Jul 2025, proxy-covered rows only (n=339,014) | RMSE |
|---|---|
| free-answer ladder | **392.83** |
| `MVT_TIME − AOBT_3_flt` | **428.07** |

**The proxy is 9% worse than the free answer it was claimed to beat by 39%.** Out of sample the
proxy degrades from 384.8 to 428.1, and worst in July (465.7 against January's 376.8). Every
sentence in §1 and §5 built on "384.8 with a single subtraction" is withdrawn. **The real
starting point is the ladder, and on the evaluation file it is 629.1, not 627.7** — because the
finest rung keys on off-block hour, and off-block is 100% null there, so that rung never fires
in production. Re-anchor it to take-off hour or delete it.

### 7.2 Step 2 was going to fail on its own arithmetic

Blended as written, proxy where available and ladder elsewhere:

| held-out fold | ladder | Step 2 as planned |
|---|---|---|
| Jan+Jul | 627.7 | **650.0** |
| Feb+Aug | 505.8 | 533.7 |
| Mar+Sep | 442.6 | 499.6 |

Worse than the ladder in all three folds. Its stated success target of roughly 385 was
unreachable, and its kill condition would have fired on day two at a 69% gap, halting the plan
and blaming a harness that was working correctly. It would also have submitted **177 negative
taxi times**, minimum −11,519 seconds. **Step 2 is replaced by: submit the ladder, clipped
positive, as the calibration baseline.**

### 7.3 The step order was backwards. The stratum is the whole game.

Measured end to end on held-out folds, take-off-anchored features only, encodings fitted inside
the fold:

| | Jan+Jul | Feb+Aug | Mar+Sep |
|---|---|---|---|
| ladder | 627.7 | 505.8 | 442.6 |
| Step 3 alone | 553.8 | 456.2 | 398.8 |
| **Step 3 + Step 4** | **371.0** | **324.9** | **268.9** |
| — covered rows alone | **260.0** | 265.3 | 228.9 |
| — unmatched stratum alone | 2,174 | 1,931 | 1,471 |

**Step 4 is worth −183 seconds; Step 3 is worth −74.** The largest lever was scheduled fourth
and labelled "kill: none, this is arithmetic". It is not arithmetic: giving the stratum its own
conditional mean buys **0.7%**, while fitting it a model buys **46%**. And it needs its own
feature set, because **every `*_flt` column is 100% null within that stratum** — operator,
market segment and weight class, all three listed in Step 4, do not exist for the rows Step 4 is
for.

**The decisive number:** on covered rows alone the model already reaches **260.0**, against a
leaderboard leader at 260.9 over all rows. **The distance between us and the top of the table is
almost entirely the 1.5% unmatched stratum.** That is where the competition is won.

### 7.4 Step 3 works, but not for the stated reason

The residual is provably `AOBT_3_flt − BLOCK_TIME_UTC_mvt`, the two organisations' disagreement
about off-block. But it is **harder** to explain than the target, not easier: airport by runway
by stand by hour explains 26.6% of the residual against 49.5% of the target, and it carries 85%
of the target's variance. It still helps, because the model sees the proxy alongside every other
feature. Do not let the "easier function" rationale drive feature selection.

### 7.5 The 2026 stratum is 44% larger than the training pool

| | 2025 all | 2025 Jan+Jul | **2026 eval** |
|---|---|---|---|
| unmatched rate | 1.066% | 1.545% | **1.534%** |
| proxy sd | 377.1 | 427.9 | **444.2** |
| proxy p99.9 | 3,600 | 3,872 | **4,661** |

Up at all ten airports; Schiphol goes 1.65% to 3.16%. Every stratum-sized estimate here is
understated, and January-and-July 2025 is the right analogue, not the all-months pool.

### 7.6 Revised order

1. **Eligibility floor** — git, GPLv3, README, amendment. Day 1. Blocks the prize, not the score.
2. **The unmatched-stratum model** — largest lever, its own features. Was step 4.
3. **The main model** with the proxy as a feature and the residual target. Was step 3.
4. **Ladder submitted once, clipped positive**, purely to confirm local and remote agree.
5. **Three congestion features**, take-off anchored, hard five-day cap.
6. **Ten-seed averaging**, a measured 3%.
7. Weather and ensembling: **deleted**.

## 8. Protocol B, remaining findings · 2026-09-08

### 8.1 A data discovery: the label is externally contradicted on the rows that decide the score

Of the 202 rows above three hours, 186 are inside the unmatched stratum, and **88.17% of those
have `BLOCK_TIME_UTC_mvt` within sixty seconds of `SCHED_TIME_UTC_mvt`** — median gap one second
— against a 9.57% base rate. On the 16 tail rows where the Network Manager clock exists, it
records an ordinary taxi-out, median 1,138 seconds, against an airport-recorded three to six
hours.

The reading: on those rows the airport never recorded a real off-block, the field carries the
**scheduled** push, and the "taxi-out" is take-off minus a schedule for a flight that departed
hours late. **That is a different variable, not a long taxi-out.**

Amendment 1 retired this concern on the grounds that the label was "internally exact". That check
verifies `TAXITIME == MVT − BLOCK`, which holds *by construction* and has **zero power** against a
wrong off-block. The concern was retired by a test that cannot fail on the failure mode.
Consequence: C3's stated justification, that long taxi-outs are genuine at congested airports, is
contradicted on every row where it can be checked. And the rows are identifiable before
prediction, from the null flag and the schedule-match, so this is a **prediction** problem, not a
robustness one.

### 8.2 The two strata are the same rows counted twice

| stratum | rows | share of squared error |
|---|---|---|
| unmatched | 22,219 | 42.22% |
| tail above three hours | 202 | 38.10% |
| **intersection** | **186** | **36.56%** |
| unmatched excluding the tail | 22,033 | **5.66%** |

§2 presented 42.2% and 38% as two separate levers. They are one. **The rationale for Step 4 was
wrong.** Its measured value, however, is real and independently confirmed at −183 seconds end to
end, so the step stands while its stated reason does not.

### 8.3 The finest ladder rung is dead on the evaluation file

`_hour` derives from off-block, which is 100% null there. `Ladder.predict` on the real submission
rows returns predictions **identical** to the no-hour rung. **The published denominator of 627.7
is not achievable on the submission set; the deliverable is 629.1.** Re-anchor hour to take-off,
which is present, or delete the rung. Additionally the best rung was selected *on the held-out
set*, so even 629.1 is mildly optimistic.

### 8.4 Step 2's gate was inside the sampling noise

Bootstrap over an evaluation-shaped scoring set: full-set RMSE 654.31 with a 95% interval of
[566, 739], **±13.2%**. The gate was "stop if local and leaderboard disagree by more than 5%."
The sampling error is 2.6 times the threshold. **No pass/fail threshold may be narrower than the
interval of the quantity it tests.** Every reported RMSE now carries a bootstrap interval.

### 8.5 The tail is a variance problem, not a training-policy problem

The optimal constant is the full mean. Excluding the 202 tail rows from *fitting* is worth
**0.006 seconds**. Predicting the median instead costs **5.74 seconds**, roughly a thousand times
more — so the framing "robustness decision" invites the one move that actually costs, reaching
for a robust loss. Under squared error the optimal prediction is the conditional mean and nothing
else. The 38% figure is a statement about **variance of the final score**, not about censoring:
six rows above 87,000 seconds sit in the 2025 January-and-July window, and any one of them
consumes about a third of the entire error budget implied by the leader's score. Placement is
partly a lottery over how many such rows fell into the 2026 evaluation months.

### 8.6 My own test suite is weaker than I claimed

Hand-run mutations that leave the suite **green**: dropping the take-off null check from C2;
flipping C3's comparison to inclusive; moving the label-identity check after C1, which is the
exact ordering the code's comment claims to guarantee; changing p99.9 to p99, which would
silently falsify a published number; `min_count` from 30 to 3, which the headline depends on;
the positive clamp on predictions, which is decoration since it is never reached. `upload()` in
the S3 client — **the function that puts the submission in the bucket — has no tests at all.**

Worse, `test_deeper_rungs_reduce_error_on_held_out_rows`, the test validating the ladder's
central claim, splits with alternating rows on a synthetic frame — an interleaved random split,
the exact discipline this pre-registration forbids elsewhere.

### 8.7 The eight tests to write before any model is fitted

Ranked by value per hour:

1. **`test_stratum_prevalence_is_stationary`** — fires RED today. January's unmatched rate goes
   0.92% in 2025 to **1.61%** in 2026 while July falls 2.05% to 1.48%. The pooled rate barely
   moves, which hides a complete regime change in the stratum that decides the score. This is the
   only 2026-side signal available without spending a submission.
2. **`test_every_feature_is_computable_on_the_ranking_file`** — catches the whole train-serve
   skew class, including the live hour defect.
3. **`test_submission_contract`**, run on the artifact before every upload: row count exactly
   344,841, identifier set equality against the current template, zero nulls, dtype read from the
   parquet schema rather than pandas, values finite and in range, re-read from disk after write.
4. **The three-probe planted-leak harness** with a measured detection floor, a negative control,
   and an assertion that the planted column actually reached the model with non-zero importance.
   A leak test that never fed the leak to the model produces the same green as a working one.
5. **`test_strata_are_disjoint_before_being_summed`** — no two strata may be reported additively
   when their intersection exceeds 5% of either.
6. **`test_block_time_is_not_the_schedule`** — fires at 88% against a 9.6% base rate today.
7. Bootstrap intervals on every reported RMSE.
8. **`test_unimpeded_refuses_a_frame_containing_holdout_rows`** — the fold guard belongs at the
   consuming boundary, which this pre-registration already mandates and the module does not
   implement.
