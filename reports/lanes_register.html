Corrected since the first version of this page

An audit run overnight (plans/TOP_PATH_2026_09_10.md) and a parallel session's work overturned four things this page previously stated as fact. They are corrected in place below, and listed here so nobody carries the old versions forward:

  • "The leader's matched RMSE is about 164.6 s" — withdrawn. One total score is one equation in two unknown stratum errors; that figure assumed the leader's unmatched error equals ours.
  • "The mode is not identifiable from any column in the file" — withdrawn. Low AUC from individual fingerprints does not show joint unpredictability. Arm F, whose screen AUC was 0.53–0.59, went on to gain +12.2 s on exactly those rows as a model feature.
  • "78% of disagreeing rows are aircraft pushed back and then held" — restated. The data shows the airport's stamp is earlier than the Network Manager's on 78% of them. Holding is an interpretation of that sign, not an observation of the aircraft.
  • "The 24-hour tail rows share no signature" — superseded. They do: the actual clock time stamped with the schedule's date, so the label is the taxi time plus exactly 86,400 s.

Intervals were too narrow for one lane. Every interval here resampled rows independently. Re-drawn by resampling whole dates (Result 23), the record-ordering arm's interval is 4.6× wider — its gain sits on the disagreeing rows, which cluster on days — while the other lanes widen about 1.5× and the blends barely move. Every verdict survives; the record-ordering gain is simply less precisely known than the first version of this page implied.

The same audit found a validation defect in the fold harness: target encodings for the early-stopping rows were fitted on those rows' own labels, so the number of trees was chosen against a biased signal for every arm and every shipped version. Held-out scores and every paired interval on this page remain valid — the held-out months were never touched — but the models behind them were not tuned as cleanly as their verdicts implied.

The one rule that has held every time

A paired fold gain on matched rows, converted to MSE, lands on the board at roughly one-to-one — 1.17× then 0.97×. That transfer is of paired differences; it does not establish that absolute fold errors transfer, and fold totals demonstrably do not (two Rome rows once carried 27% of one).

Diagnosis

Where the error actually lives

The target is the airport's off-block stamp subtracted from take-off. A second clock — the Network Manager's AOBT_3 — anchors the prediction. When the two clocks agree we predict well. Almost half the error sits in the 7.6% of rows where they disagree by more than ten minutes; on 78% of those the airport's stamp is the earlier one.

  • Individually, nothing in the file names those rows well — the best single signals reach AUC 0.55–0.63. Jointly, something does: the record-ordering block moved them by +12.2 s as a model feature. The screens were the wrong test for the question.
  • Among rows identical in airport, stand, runway and hour the clock difference still spreads 197 s in the body and 939 s on the disagreeing rows. That bounds a model restricted to those four columns; our model already beats it, so it is not a floor for ours.

Now

What is running, and what changed the arithmetic

v6 is fitting — arm F on top of v5, due around 13:15. A parallel session owns that fit and the work after it; the machine died on battery at 10:50 and the fit was relaunched at 10:59.

The picture changed overnight, and not because of my arms. The parallel session located the gap against a public, GPLv3-licensed 271-scoring system and found it sits almost entirely in our unmatched rows at Rome. Its arm R2 — a stake-weighted fill classifier plus the 24-hour date-slip class — passed every registered clause on the fold, with a projected ~4,150 MSE. That is a fold projection, not a board result, and its date-slip half is disclosed as boundary-fragile.

Against a 7,350 MSE gap: arm F (~745, established) and R2 (~4,150, projected) are the first pair of lanes whose sum is the same order of magnitude as the distance to tenth. They were measured separately and cannot simply be added; the board is the instrument.

Method

What this record got wrong, and what caught it

Each threshold was written into an append-only pre-registration before its measurement existed, so no result was rescued by moving its bar. That discipline held. What it did not prevent was over-reading the negatives: a lane failing as built was written up as the question being closed, and a diagnostic bound was quoted as if it were a competitor's score. The audit caught both.

Controls earned their keep twice. A permuted control keeping 71% of a gain turned Result 4 into a refutation. And a random-subset control scoring −23.9 s — where it must score about zero by construction — exposed a misread file convention that had produced a confident −109 s (Result 20a). The same bug in a lane without a control would have been reported as a finding.
